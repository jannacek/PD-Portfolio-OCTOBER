Synthesis

What the four AT-LABs together support, what they only suggest, and what has still to be tested before the final assessment.

Four labs, three lenses, one exploratory vocabulary. The question for this page is not “what has been proved?” but “what does each contribution stand on, and what would strengthen it?”

The answer is uneven by design. The four environments were built for their own settings and left evidence of different kinds. Some contributions are well supported, some are supported only at the level of design, and a few are claims I name as open. Each is tagged with one of three statuses used across the portfolio.

Three connected layers

The layers are connected but not equally developed. The first generates the material, the second reads it, the third sits beside both.

Layer 1 · Format

The AT-LAB

A recurring structure for artistic research into AI and data literacy, recognised across four environments. The most developed layer, with a methods manuscript in preparation. Being consolidated
Layer 2 · Reading

Three lenses

Concept Clarity, Perceived Agency, Transferability. Sensitising concepts, anchored in one lab each and read across the others. They orient the analysis; they do not measure. Applied · not validated
Layer 3 · Vocabulary

EMI

A practitioner-facing vocabulary for the artist-researcher’s epistemic contribution inside a collaboration. A different level from the lenses, and kept apart from the methods manuscript. Exploratory · v0.3

Five contributions and what they stand on Recalibrated

01 · The AT-LAB as a translational inquiry format

A recurring six-element form, with lenses, that carries questions between very different sites and leaves findings with their sites. Recognised after the fact and refined in the writing.

Demonstrated across four environments, by its author. Designed for re-situation by others. Open a documented re-run by another facilitator.

02 · Literacy as a condition to stage, not only a goal to teach

The shift from “how do we teach AI literacy?” to “what conditions let it emerge in an encounter?” changes what is designed and what counts as evidence. It is a research stance, and the labs are its worked examples.

Demonstrated as a way of framing and designing four inquiries. Open whether any participant’s literacy changed: nothing in the portfolio measures that, and I do not claim it.

03 · Sensitising lenses as a middle layer between the local and the comparable

Three concepts that let situated material be described and juxtaposed without being turned into scores or into generic claims about literacy. They came out of practice, not out of theory.

Demonstrated in use, with an auditable analysis for AT-LAB I. Open independent coding, and use by anyone else; the reading so far is by one researcher, in retrospect.

04 · ATMOS_FERA as translational modelling

A method that treats radiative-transfer data as behavioural constraint on light, sound and spatial density, argued as a form of atmospheric knowing. The performance enacts the method; it does not demonstrate an audience effect.

Demonstrated as a documented method and a peer-reviewed argument (ISEA2026, double-blind). Open any perceptual or learning effect; the paper itself says the method needs site-specific calibration.

05 · Artistic research as an identifiable epistemic contribution in transdisciplinary work (EMI)

A vocabulary for saying what artistic research contributes inside a collaboration, in terms collaborators can follow.

Demonstrated as a working vocabulary derived from two embedded cases, presented in public on 23 April 2026 to practitioners from three consortia. Open use outside those cases and by anyone but me; it is not a validated instrument.

What stays open Updated

The trajectory does not close the question of AI and data literacy through art. It opens it more precisely. The findings are situated and partial, and the register of the claims is chosen to match. The points below are the format’s own next tests, plus the open questions the labs raise.
